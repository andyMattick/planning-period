## In one paragraph

This was the first run with every recommendation of the token audit adopted (a starter page, a shop line finder, fresh short sessions, a cheaper tier for mechanical work). The coordinating session started the log and handed the run to a 🧭 unit lead, which this time started every making role as its own short cloud session instead of doing them itself. Every check was kept: the solver checked 9,600 problems on every step, a blind solver took 288 unit-test items without the key, and fresh eyes used the real screens. ## What went wrong and was redone

- **The new pattern checks failed the shipped units.** The checks written for audit A found real sign, key-spread and equal-value patterns in the Dive Shop and Balloon Port. They were real defects, but fixing them was extra work that went into builder round 2.
- **The builder was one long session.** 26.22 million tokens and $10.03 for about 80 tool calls, roughly twice its share of the budget; the rest of the run was briefed for short sessions.
- **Three sessions failed at once on start.** They were started from a short commit id, which is not accepted ("branch or commit not found"). The coordinating session archived them, pushed a checkpoint branch and started them again.
- **The ratReason L3 giveaway got through two rounds.** The blind solver's version was fixed in round 2, but the fixed version still let her typed estimate match exactly one choice (1,000 of 1,000). Every script passed it; only fresh eyes saw it. Round 3 and a second recheck cost $2.63 and $0.93.
- **A 77% guess strategy.** On ratReason a guesser scored 77% against 25% by chance. The full fairness bot still passed; the blind solver found it.
- **"Not reasonable" claims landed close to the truth.** The solver found claims about 3% of the time (0.4% at L1) near enough to the true value to be arguable.
- **The checks were narrowed mid-run** by the owner ("only the NEW stuff, that's done weekly"): after 19:07 check-math and the fairness bot ran for the Honey Hive only.
- **Every making role cost more than planned.** Builder rounds 2 and 3 cost $5.03 and $2.63; the worksheet maker $4.78; fresh eyes $6.06 (the audit aimed for $3.70–4.30).

### Quality: every check still ran

No check was dropped (the owner narrowed the later ones to the Honey Hive, which the log records). What each caught:

- **Pattern checks** (audit A): sign, key-spread, equal-value patterns in Units 1–2.
- **Builder self-review and the fairness bot:** a −237.5% weight story, a 1-digit-block crash, fractional bees, "12 ÷ 12" frames, and 3 test-wise tells.
- **Solver:** 9,600 problems, 2 real FIX in ratReason (the close "Not reasonable" claims) and 6 judgment calls; the rechecks confirmed the fixes.
- **Blind solver:** all 288 items agreed, 4 FIX, 6 JUDGMENT; the 77% guess strategy on ratReason (25% by chance).
- **Fresh eyes:** **the ratReason L3 giveaway (typed estimate matched one choice in 1,000 of 1,000), which every script and every earlier checker passed,** plus a video listed twice and screen readers reading two bars the same.

- **A quality slip, honestly:** the ratReason L3 giveaway shipped past the builder, the solver, the blind solver and the solver recheck, and was found only by fresh eyes. **Verdict.** The audit's checks and the idea of short sessions worked; its dollar target did not hold. The run cost about 2.3–2.6 times the budget, mostly because the builder and the fresh-eyes review ran long, the worksheet maker and game designer were real costs the Unit 2 estimate had hidden, and the audit's savings (B, C, D, G) did not show up as numbers.
