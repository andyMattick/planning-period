# Unit 4, Lesson 3: Chemical Reactions and Conservation of Mass
Mr. Okafor · Science 8 · my lesson notes (made-up sample)

## Big idea
In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged into new substances. No atoms are created and none are destroyed, so the
total mass before a reaction equals the total mass after it. When it *looks* like mass changed, something got in or
got out (usually a gas). I want them saying "the atoms went somewhere," never "the mass disappeared."

## 1. Reactants and products
- **Reactants** are the substances you start with. They are written on the left side of a chemical equation.
- **Products** are the new substances that form. They are written on the right side.
- The arrow (→) means "react to form" or "yields." A plus sign separates two reactants or two products.
- Example we do on the board: hydrogen gas and oxygen gas react to form water.
  2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Reactants: H₂ and O₂. Product: H₂O.
- Students mix these up when the equation is written in words. Say it out loud: "left is what we start with."

## 2. Signs of a chemical change
A chemical change makes a new substance. Clues that one may have happened:
1. A gas forms that wasn't there before (bubbles or fizzing, when you didn't just heat a liquid to boiling).
2. A solid forms when two clear liquids are mixed (a **precipitate**).
3. An unexpected color change.
4. A temperature change without heating or cooling it from outside (it gets warm or cold by itself).
5. Light or a new odor is given off.

One clue alone is not proof. Boiling water makes bubbles, but it is still water: that's a physical change
(a change of state). Ice melting, sugar dissolving, cutting paper, and crushing a can are physical changes, because no
new substance forms. Rusting iron, baking a cake, burning wood and a glow stick lighting up are chemical changes.

## 3. Reading a formula and counting atoms
- The **subscript** (small number after a symbol) tells how many atoms of that element are in one molecule.
  H₂O has 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom. No subscript means 1.
- The **coefficient** (big number in front) tells how many molecules. 3H₂O means 3 molecules of water:
  3 × 2 = 6 hydrogen atoms and 3 × 1 = 3 oxygen atoms.
- Parentheses: Ca(OH)₂ has 1 calcium, 2 oxygen and 2 hydrogen atoms (the 2 applies to everything inside).
- We always count with a table: element, atoms on the reactant side, atoms on the product side.

## 4. Balancing simple equations
- A **balanced** equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides. That's conservation of mass
  written in symbols.
- Balance by changing **coefficients only**. Never change a subscript: that changes the substance (H₂O₂ is hydrogen
  peroxide, not water).
- Steps: count atoms in a table → pick an element that doesn't match → change a coefficient → recount everything →
  repeat until every row matches.
- Practice equations from class:
  - 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
  - CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O (methane burning)
  - N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃
  - 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
  - 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ (rust; the challenge one)
- Kids love to "balance" by writing a 2 as a subscript. Catch it every time.

## 5. Closed vs. open systems
- A **closed system** lets nothing in or out (a sealed flask, a zip bag, a bottle with a balloon stretched over the
  top). The mass on the balance stays the same before and after the reaction.
- An **open system** lets matter in or out (an open cup or beaker). A gas product can escape, so the balance shows
  less mass after. A gas from the air (like oxygen) can join a product, so the balance can show *more* mass after.
- Our lab: baking soda + vinegar.
  - In an open cup: 150.0 g before, about 148.9 g after. The carbon dioxide gas left the cup.
  - In a sealed zip bag: 150.0 g before, 150.0 g after. The bag puffs up because the CO₂ is trapped inside.
  - The equation (I show it, they don't have to memorize it):
    NaHCO₃ + HC₂H₃O₂ → NaC₂H₃O₂ + H₂O + CO₂
- Demo: steel wool burning on a balance gets *heavier*, because iron atoms join with oxygen atoms from the air.
  Mass of steel wool + mass of oxygen used = mass of the iron oxide made.
- Mass word problem pattern: mass of reactants = mass of products. If 10.0 g of A reacts with 6.0 g of B and only
  one product forms, 16.0 g of product forms. If a gas escapes from an open container, its mass = mass before − mass
  after.

## Common mistakes I see every year
- "The mass disappeared" / "the gas has no mass." Gas has mass.
- Changing subscripts to balance.
- Thinking a balanced equation means the same number of *molecules* on each side (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O is 3 molecules
  → 2 molecules, and it's balanced).
- Calling any bubbles a chemical change.
- Using "weight" and "mass" as if they mean the same; in this unit we measure mass in grams on a balance.
