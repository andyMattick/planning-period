## 4. The probe's line, and why

**A version fails when its multiple choice is right more than chance + 15 points** (4 choices: more than 40%; 8 mc: 4
right fails, 10 mc: 5 fails), **or a short answer earns more than half its credit without the passage.** Any item right
at confidence 4–5 from a named cue is a FIX either way.
- It is where the owner's line already sat (25% + 15 = 40%), now with a reason, and it is Pet World's margin for a
  check a model runs (exact scripted bots get 10 points, as Wilco does).
- A probe that only guesses clears it 89% of the time on 8 items and 92% on 10 (binomial, p = 0.25); on 6, 7 or 9 items
  only 76–83%. So the default now keeps **at least 8 mc a version**: the remedy for noise is more items, never a higher
  line.
- A false fail costs one FIX round on the named items (about $2.75 + $0.54); a false pass sends out a quiz that doesn't
  test reading. Both old samples would fail it by a wide margin (9–10 of 10).
- Short answers can't be guessed by chance, so their line is credit: an answer written without the passage that
  earns over half fails the version.

## 5. The new automated check: `checks/no-passage.mjs`

Bots that read one version's stems and choices together, never the passage: **pairMatch** (lines up Part B's quotes
with Part A's choices by shared words), **echo** (the choice whose words come back in the version's other items),
**quoteEcho** (a three-word run printed in another item) and **readingVote**. Wilco's rule (more than 10 points above
chance, groups of 8+). `--self-test` builds a clean and a leaky reading quiz and a no-passage quiz (all three behave);
`npm run check` runs the self-test and every sample with reading items.

**Result on the current samples:** both fail, as expected for packs made before option 1.
- g7-ela: version B readingVote 40% (chance 25%); A passes (pairMatch 35%).
- g7-ela2: version B echo 38%; **A passes** (worst echo 28%), though fresh probes got A 9 of 10. The bots catch the
  cross-item part only; A's leaks were general knowledge and "the key sounds right", which only the probe sees. That is
  why the probe stays the gate.
- Every other sample has no reading items and is skipped.

So `npm run check` still passes for unrelated work, the two are on **`checks/no-passage-debt.json`, a known-failing list
that can only shrink**: `npm run check` fails if a pack on it now passes (take it off), if a pack off it fails, or if the
list names anything outside `NO_PASSAGE_DEBT_MAX` in `scripts/check.mjs` (the two old samples). Nothing was weakened:
the bots' line is Wilco's. **The samples need the option-1 remake; I did not remake them.**

## 6. The maker's dollar cap, with the arithmetic

**A first draft gets one third of the order's budget (tokens × $0.10), a redesign one quarter, a FIX round one sixth,
and never more than half of what is left below the 2× cost stop.** Reading quiz + rubric (165 tokens, $16.50):
**$5.50, $4.10, $2.75.** (`claude -p --max-budget-usd`; runner at least 60 minutes.)
- Every logged maker session outside g7-ela2 fits: g4-math $1.38 of $2.33; g8-science $2.02 of $3.83; g11-history
  $3.01 of $3.17; g7-ela's first draft $3.06 of $5.50 at the reading price; every FIX round under its sixth (largest:
  g7-ela r4 $2.61 of $2.75).
- g7-ela2's would have been cut: $7.32 → $5.50 and $12.21 → $4.10 ($9.93 less), and its capped round 3 ($2.42 of
  $5.50) shows a cap works. The cost of a cut is an incomplete draft: the maker now saves `pack.json` as it goes, and
  one session at the FIX cap finishes it before the owner is told.
- A reading quiz with a rubric then costs about **$7.6–17.0 with no redesign** (low: maker $3.0, two probes $0.54, the
  passage fact check $0.3, blind $1.0, FIX $1.1, recheck $0.5, changed-item probes $0.27, final probes $0.54, reporter
  $0.35; high: makers at $5.50 and $2.75, fact check $1.2, blind $2.7, recheck $2.7, probes $1.62, reporter $0.5),
  **plus $4.64 per redesign**.
