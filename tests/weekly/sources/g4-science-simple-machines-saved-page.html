How Simple Machines Make Work Easier

By the KidSci Daily team (made-up) · Grade 4 reading level

A machine is anything that makes work easier. In science, work means using a force to move an object over a distance. Simple machines have few or no moving parts, and they help us by changing the size or the direction of a force.

There are six simple machines: the lever, the wheel and axle, the pulley, the inclined plane, the wedge and the screw.

Lever

A lever is a stiff bar that turns on a fixed point called the fulcrum. A seesaw is a lever. Pushing down on one end of a crowbar lifts a heavy object at the other end.

Wheel and axle

A wheel and axle is a wheel attached to a smaller rod, the axle, so they turn together. A doorknob and a bicycle pedal crank are examples.

Pulley

A pulley is a wheel with a rope around it. Pulling down on the rope of a flagpole pulley raises the flag: the pulley changes the direction of the force.

Inclined plane

An inclined plane is a flat, slanted surface, like a ramp. Pushing a box up a ramp takes less force than lifting it straight up, but you push it a longer distance.

Wedge

A wedge is two inclined planes back to back. It pushes things apart. An axe and a doorstop are wedges.

Screw

A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a post. Turning a jar lid or a screw moves it in or out a little with each turn.

Many tools are compound machines, made of two or more simple machines. A pair of scissors uses two levers and two wedges.

Try it: look around your classroom and find three simple machines. Which one changes the direction of a force?
